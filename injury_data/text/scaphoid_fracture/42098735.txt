[FULLTEXT: Results] The study sample consisted of 15 men and 2 women, with a mean age of 30.5 years (range, 20 to 45 years). Clinical follow-up was conducted at a mean of 26 months (range, 24–30 months), during which we recorded radiographic union and return to activity. Bony union was confirmed in 15 of 17 patients (88%) at a mean of 15.4 weeks, based on clinical examination and standard radiographic assessment. According to the Mayo Modified Wrist Score, 11 patients achieved excellent outcomes and 4 achieved good outcomes. Overall, 15 of 17 patients (88%) returned to their pre-injury level of work or sports activities.
[FULLTEXT: Results] The study followed 17 athletes over an average period of 26 months (ranging from 24 to 30 months). The mean duration from injury to surgery was 8.5 months (ranging from 4 to 25 months). Among the injuries, 5 occurred in the nondominant hands, and 12 were work-related. Using the Lichtman classification, nonunion were classified as stage I in 6 patients and stage II in 11 patients. Additionally, 16 patients exhibited scaphoid waist nonunion, while the remaining one had proximal pole nonunion. Radiographic alignment, including scapholunate, radiolunate, and lateral intra-scaphoid angles, significantly improved among stage I and II patients.
