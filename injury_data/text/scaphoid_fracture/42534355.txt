[FULLTEXT: 2.5. Surgical Follow‐Up] The patient received 2 g of cefazolin within 1 h before incision for perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis. Postoperative QuickDASH (Quick Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand) scores were recorded longitudinally. At 1 week, radiographs showed no interval changes. The patient reported substantial improvement in pain, and the incision was well healed. He remained nonweight‐bearing; the thumb spica splint was removed, and a short arm cast was applied. At 1 month, the cast was removed, and the patient was transitioned to a wrist brace without activity restrictions. Protruding suture material was noted at the surgical site without drainage. This was considered most consistent with a localized reaction to suture material rather than a true surgical‐site infection. As a precaution, a 10‐day course of trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole was prescribed. QuickDASH scores continued to improve. By 2 months, the suture abscess had resolved; the patient reported complete resolution of wrist pain and functional limitation, and radiographs confirmed progressive osseous remodeling consistent with scaphoid healing (Table 1). This case illustrates the diagnostic challenges posed by OO in the scaphoid. The patient′s age, history of trauma, and MRI findings initially led to misdiagnosis as a scaphoid fracture. Dedicated CT ultimately established the diagnosis by demonstrating a characteristic 5‐mm nidus with central mineralization in the distal scaphoid.
