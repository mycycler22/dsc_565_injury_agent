No recurrent instability occurred following EAR-DL, compared with 4 (15.4%) cases in the conservative group (p = 0.126). The return to sports was observed at an average of 33.04 ± 3.18 weeks in the conservative group and at 29.32 ± 1.60 weeks in the EAR-DL group (p < 0.001).
[FULLTEXT: Results] The study included 45 patients; 26 (58%) of them were in the conservative group, while 19 (42%) were in the EAR-DL group. The study included patients, of which 18 (40.0%) were women and 27 (60.0%) were men. The average age of those receiving conservative treatment was 35.85 ± 11.63, while those performing EAR-DL had an average age of 34.84 ± 12.20. The mean follow-up duration was 15.23 ± 2.45 months in the conservative group, while it was 15.16 ± 2.01 months in the EAR-DL group (p = 0.916). While recurrent instability occurred in 4 patients (15.4%) in the conservative group, no cases of recurrent instability were observed in the EAR-DL group (0%). However, the difference between the groups was not statistically significant (p = 0.126). In the 19 patients who underwent EAR-DL, the primary indications were as follows: in 6 patients (31.6%), deltoid ligament fibers extending into the joint space and preventing reduction; in 8 patients (42.1%), inadequate stability following percutaneous screw fixation of medial avulsion fragments larger than 5 mm due to impaired local bone quality related to the fracture; in 1 patient (5.3%), a concomitant talar osteochondral lesion; and in 4 patients (21.0%), high functional expectations. Among the 6 patients whose deltoid ligament fibers extended into the joint space, 1 also had a high level of functional expectations. An ATFL injury was present in 2 (7.7%) of the patients who had conservative treatment, while it was observed in 1 (5.3%) of those who performed EAR-DL (p = 0.999). No talus osteochondral defects were observed in any of the patients who received conservative treatment, whereas 1 (5.3%) patient in the EAR-DL group had such a defect (p = 0.422). The tibial fleck sign was present in 3 (11.5%) of the patients in the conservative group and in 1 (5.3%) of those who were treated with EAR-DL (p = 0.627). 32 (71.1%) patients also had syndesmotic ligament injury and were treated with syndesmosis screws. In the conservative group, 19 (73.1%) patients had a syndesmotic injury, as did 13 (68.4%) patients who were treated with EAR-DL (p = 0.994). In the conservative group, 4 (15.4%) had open fractures, while in the EAR-DL group, 2 (10.5%) had open fractures (p = 0.999). An external fixator was performed on 7 (26.9%) of the patients in the conservative group and on 5 (26.3%) of those who were treated with EAR-DL; however, this surgical procedure did not result in a statistically significant difference between the groups (p = 0.999). In the conservative group, a fibula fracture was observed in 22 (84.6%) patients, while in the EAR-DL group, a fibula fracture was observed in 18 (94.7%) patients, and the fibula fracture ranked first among the accompanying injuries (p = 0.378). A medial malleolus fracture was observed in 16 (61.5%) patients in the conservative group, while it was observed in 12 (63.2%) patients in the EAR-DL group (p = 0.999). A Volkman fracture was observed in 6 (23.1%) patients in the conservative group, while it was observed in 7 (36.8%) patients in the EAR-DL group (p = 0.501). Proximal tibiofibular joint dislocation was observed in 3 (11.5%) patients in the conservative group and in 3 (15.8%) patients in the EAR-DL group (p = 0.686). In terms of complications, 2 (10.5%) patients who had EAR-DL procedures experienced pain and a tingling sensation along the superficial peroneal nerve tract following ankle arthroscopy; however, these symptoms completely resolved by the end of the first year (p = 0.173). In the conservative group, 2 (7.7%) patients and in the EAR-DL group, 1 (5.3%) patient developed superficial wound infections related to ankle fracture surgery; however, these fully resolved with wound dressing changes and oral antibiotic therapy (p = 0.999).
