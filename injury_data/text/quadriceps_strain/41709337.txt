[FULLTEXT: Results] A total of 116 quadriceps muscle injuries were sustained among 89 NBA players. Contusion was the most common injury type, and the majority of injuries resulted in less than ten games missed. PER at 1 year after injury (15.8; P = 0.015) was significantly lower than baseline PER (16.5), but recovered at 2 years post-injury (16.8; P = 0.166). Older age was associated with a relative risk of having a decreased USG% (relative risk [RR] 0.83; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.76–0.90) and a decreased PER (RR 0.75; 95% CI 0.64–0.88) at 1 year after injury, but these associations were not significant at 2 years after injury (P = 0.084 and P = 0.431, respectively).
[FULLTEXT: Outcome measures] Inclusion criteria includes any active NBA player that experienced a quadriceps muscle injury in the study period stated above, with at least 30 days of follow-up, which was demonstrated by having active inputs in the databases mentioned. The key word, “quadriceps” was required to be in the name of the injury in order to classify as part of this study. From the injury diagnosis given on Pro Sports Transactions, injuries were classified as contusion, soreness, strain, and muscle tear, based on what was stated in the injury description. The injuries were also characterized based on severity, where “mild” means 1 game missed, “moderate” means 2–10 games missed, and “severe” means greater than 10 games missed. Furthermore, the number of quadriceps muscle injuries that were sustained in a single season was recorded, as well as which part of the season it was recorded (regular season or post-season). Player demographics, including position, age, height, body mass index (BMI), and performance statistics were collected from Basketball-Reference. The specific performance statistics that were retrieved included points per game (PPG), minutes per game (MPG), usage percentage (USG%), and player efficiency rating (PER). USG% estimates the proportion of team plays a player is involved in while they are on the court. PER estimates a player’s productivity per minute, adjusted for pace, by accounting for both positive statistics (such as points scored, assists, rebounds, and steals) and negative statistics (such as missed shots, turnovers, and personal fouls). The scale for PER is designed so that the league average is always set at 15.0. The on-court positions include point guard, shooting guard, small forward, power forward, and center. MPG and PPG include all games (regular season and post-season) of the corresponding season. These performance statistics were recorded for the index season (season that the injury occurred), as well as the season prior (baseline), one season after, and two seasons after. Of note, injuries that occurred in seasons without a prior season or subsequent season present were excluded from the later analysis comparing performance outcomes across seasons. Furthermore, players that had multiple quadriceps muscle injuries in one season were excluded from these same analyses.
